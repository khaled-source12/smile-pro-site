<p>When considering laser vision correction to eliminate dependency on glasses or contact lenses, the term <strong>"corneal thickness"</strong> appears repeatedly. Precise measurement of corneal thickness is the bedrock requirement for determining whether you are an ideal candidate for Femto LASIK, or if your eyes require an alternative procedure such as SMILE Pro or Photorefractive Keratectomy (PRK).</p>

<p>In this detailed clinical guide, Dr. Mohamed El Naggar — Consultant Corneal and Refractive Surgeon — explains how corneal thickness is evaluated, the safety concept of Residual Stromal Bed (RSB), and the optimal solutions for thin corneas.</p>

<h2>1. The Importance of Corneal Pachymetry</h2>
<p>Corneal pachymetry is an essential component of pre-operative Pentacam mapping. Measured in microns (micrometers), this test ensures that the cornea possesses sufficient biomechanical strength to withstand laser reshaping without compromising stability or risking secondary corneal ectasia.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Average Corneal Thickness:</strong> A normal central corneal thickness ranges from <strong>500 to 580 microns</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Ablation Depth:</strong> Each diopter of myopic correction typically removes approximately 12 to 15 microns of stromal tissue.</li>
<li><strong>Residual Stromal Bed (RSB) Rule:</strong> Refractive guidelines mandate leaving an intact stromal bed of at least <strong>250 to 300 microns</strong> under the flap for lifelong biomechanical stability.</li>
</ul>

<div class="highlight-box">
<strong>💡 Safety Formula:</strong> Total Thickness - (Flap Thickness + Laser Tissue Removed) = Residual Stromal Bed (RSB must be ≥ 250 - 300 microns).
</div>

<h2>2. When is Femto LASIK the Ideal Choice?</h2>
<p>Femto LASIK uses a femtosecond laser to create an ultra-thin corneal flap (100–110 microns), followed by excimer laser ablation to reshape the underlying stroma. To safely undergo Femto LASIK, patients should meet the following criteria:</p>
<ul>
<li>Total central corneal thickness of at least <strong>500 microns</strong>.</li>
<li>Balanced refractive error leaving an RSB comfortably greater than 300 microns.</li>
<li>No early signs of corneal weakness or subclinical keratoconus on Pentacam maps.</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. Best Alternatives for Thin Corneas</h2>
<p>When diagnostic imaging reveals a thinner-than-average cornea (e.g., 470–500 microns) or high myopia requiring significant tissue removal, Dr. Mohamed El Naggar recommends alternative procedures designed to preserve corneal tissue:</p>

<h3>1. SMILE Pro & Femto SMILE</h3>
<p>SMILE Pro is an outstanding option for thin corneas or high prescriptions because it is a <strong>flapless procedure</strong>. A femtosecond laser creates a tiny lenticule within the intact stroma, which is extracted through a keyhole incision of just 2 mm.</p>
<p>By avoiding a large corneal flap, the strong anterior corneal layers (Bowman's layer) remain intact, allowing higher corrections while maximizing biomechanical stability.</p>

<h3>2. Surface Laser (PRK / TransPRK)</h3>
<p>PRK is a premier choice for borderline or thin corneas. The laser reshapes the surface of the cornea directly after gently removing the epithelial layer. Because no flap or deep stromal cut is made, the entire stromal thickness remains available for correction, preserving maximum tissue.</p>

<h2>5. How Dr. Mohamed El Naggar Recommends the Right Procedure</h2>
<p>Surgical candidacy is never based on corneal thickness alone. Dr. Mohamed El Naggar conducts a comprehensive assessment incorporating:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Corneal Topography & Elevation Maps:</strong> To verify anterior and posterior surface regularity.</li>
<li><strong>Refractive Error Magnitudes:</strong> Balancing prescription strength against available tissue thickness.</li>
<li><strong>Age and Lifestyle Needs:</strong> Factoring in occupational demands, active sports, and visual expectations.</li>
</ul>
